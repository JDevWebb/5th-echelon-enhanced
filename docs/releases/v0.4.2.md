Switching servers is one click, the launcher shows what it's doing in one place, tells you when your server updates or goes down for maintenance, shows the roadmap and takes your suggestions, and makes your account for you. Joins that fail are easier to fix, reports with logs get through, and the launcher finds a server when the coordinator is down.

## Play

Already on 0.4.1? The launcher updates itself. New here? Download **`launcher.exe`** (Windows) or **`launcher-linux-x86_64`** (Linux, Steam Deck) below, run it, press **Connect**, then **Play**. More in [Play in five minutes](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced#play-in-five-minutes).

## New: the game's diagnostics

**The first time you start this version, the launcher asks you about something new, before the game runs.** 5th Echelon can now send the server you play on a short diagnostic log while you play: the game's warnings, errors and network messages, a few lines a minute at most, never the whole log. It lets the server's admins see why a join failed or a connection dropped on your side, not just theirs.

Before anything leaves your PC, your PC's name, your Windows account name, your user folder and your internet address are removed. It goes only to the server you're playing on and is kept for at most 30 days. **Show me an example** shows the lines from your own last game that would have been sent. You choose **Keep sending** or **Turn it off**; nothing is sent until you've answered, and **Settings › Feedback** changes it any time.

## What's new

### Playing
- **Different game data refuses at the lobby again.** Players whose game files differ (a mod such as a loose `PEC.ini`) can't start a match together, so the host refuses them at the lobby with the game's own "different version" message instead of letting them in to fail when the match starts. Players with the same mods play together fine.
- **The overlay has a mouse in play, too.** Opened outside the SMI map (F5), its cursor was hidden and the mouse still turned the camera. Now the overlay shows its own cursor, the cursor moves freely in the game's window, and the game gets no mouse or keys until the panel closes.
- **Signing in again after a drop or a restart works every time, and leaves no dead lobbies behind.** A game that lost the server, or was started again, signs in from the same address and port while the server still holds its old connection for a minute. Lobbies the old game made stayed in friends' lists for as long as the player was online, and joining one failed; they now go when the old connection does. And about 1 in 256 such sign-ins within a few seconds failed outright, because the server took the new sign-in for a resend of the old one.
- **Large requests from the game** (a big stats request, say) no longer fail when their pieces arrive out of order.
- **Your ping to the server** is on the overlay's network line (F5), measured every 20 seconds.
- **A warning before maintenance.** From 10 minutes before your server (or the whole network) goes down for booked maintenance, the overlay shows how long is left, for how long, and the admins' note, so you can finish your match.
- **A warning when other players can't reach you.** If the server's NAT helper never hears from your game, or stops hearing from it, matches with other players can't connect, and until now nothing said so. The overlay now shows **Other players can't reach you** at the top of the screen with the reason and what to do (a firewall or antivirus blocking the game's traffic to the server, usually), and it goes away by itself once the server hears from the game again. With internet play set to **LAN or VPN only**, the overlay says so when the game starts.

### The launcher
- **Roadmap:** a new screen with what's shipping, what's next and what's later for 5th Echelon, and a form to **suggest a feature or improvement**. Your suggestions stay listed there with the admins' answer.
- **Maintenance warnings:** on the day your server (or the network) is booked for maintenance, or from 12 hours before, a notice under **Play** says when and for how long, in your own time, with the admins' note; the server menu marks that server. From 30 minutes before it offers a server that stays up, and while yours is down, **Play** waits and offers it.
- **What's new, once after each update:** the main changes you'll see and the fixes you'd notice, with the ones only the community network has marked, and a way back to it if you play elsewhere. Missed it? Click the version at the bottom of the side menu.
- **GitHub and Discord in the side menu,** at the bottom: the project's page for releases and issues, and the developer's Discord (right-click to copy the name).
- **Who runs your server:** the server card's caption says **Self-hosted** for a server on no network (your own, a friend's, a LAN party's), or the other network's name for another group's; hover it for what that means. Community servers show nothing new.
- **A server menu on the Play screen.** The server card opens the community servers, best first, with your ping and the players on each. Pick one and you're switched there at once, with your friends and name. A server of your own, a LAN party's or another group's network is on the **Servers** screen, with your recent servers and one click back to the community network.
- **One activity bar** along the bottom for everything that takes a while, and every error: switching servers (with its steps, and how long each took, under **Steps**), creating your account, sending feedback (with how much has gone up; the window closes as soon as you press **Send**), installing, the connection test and updates. Something that fails stays until you close it, with **Try again**, **Copy details**, and for a report, **Without logs**.
- **Your account is made for you** on a community server you haven't played on: with the name you use everywhere, no questions. You're asked only if someone there already has it (an account from before names were kept for each player), with the name checked as you type and free ones to pick.
- **When your server updates:** while a new release goes out to the servers, a notice under the launch bar says when yours updates and is back, in your own time zone, and that you can keep playing until then. While it restarts, it offers a server already on the new release; and if your server runs a newer version than your launcher, **Update and restart**.
- **A warning when the game's data is modified**, naming the mod when it's a known one (the balaclava mod), with how to play with everyone.
- **Servers without the coordinator:** the launcher remembers the last server list and knows the community's three servers, so you can still find and join one when the coordinator is down.
- **No more `bl-dataversion.txt`:** the game no longer writes what its data version is made of, and the launcher deletes the file left from earlier versions. It can be turned back on, for working out a "different version of the game", under **Settings › Advanced › Hooks**.
- **Bans and sign-in limits say why.** A banned account shows as **Banned on this server** in the Status card, with how long and why, and too many sign-ins says *Too many failed logins; try again later*, instead of "Error when sending request".
- **Names up to 32 characters**, as servers allow (the launcher cut them to 24).
- **Uninstall** (Settings › 5th Echelon client) says it only puts the game's DLL back: your settings, accounts, saves and identity stay, so Install brings it all back.
- **Connecting from far away is more patient.** The launcher waits up to 10 seconds for a server to say how it's set up (3 before), asks twice, and when it still gets no answer says so, instead of "*address* doesn't answer on port 50051" (a port the community servers keep closed).
- **Reports with logs get through.** A report with a few MB of logs could fail with a server error, from far away especially. The launcher now keeps reports small enough for every server, waits longer for the upload, and when it still fails, says so and offers to send without the logs.

### Running servers
- **The admin UI, redesigned** in the launcher's look: an icon rail, every page opening with its title and a band of its key numbers and actions, then cards. Bandwidth and Activity are tabs under **Network**, the audit log under **Security**. Dark only.
- **Maintenance windows:** book downtime for servers or the whole network on **Updates**, with a note for players. Launchers warn on the day (or from 12 hours before), the game warns 10 minutes before, and no update starts on a server during its window. [operations.md](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced/blob/main/docs/operations.md#maintenance-windows)
- **Roadmap and suggestions:** a **Roadmap** page to keep the project's roadmap that players see in their launchers, an inbox of the suggestions they send (reply, decline or promote to a lane), and a ready-to-paste prompt for Claude Code on every item and suggestion. [operations.md](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced/blob/main/docs/operations.md#roadmap-and-suggestions)
- **Sessions** in the admin UI: each player's time on the servers (online, in a party or match, whose and with whom), with what went wrong: refused sign-ins, failed joins (with the game's error by name), players who dropped out of a match, invitations that never arrived, games the relay couldn't reach, the game's own errors, and players on different servers who missed each other.
- **The map shows day and night as it is now,** and each player online with what they're doing: hover for their name, server, city, who they're with, how their game is reached and the time there. Legends' colours show again in the admin UI.
- **What the relay costs:** each match someone joined shows, on Sessions, both players' ping to the server and whether the relay carried their traffic; for a relayed pair, their round trip through the server next to the least a direct one could take over the distance between them. **Relayed round trip** at the top gives the median over the period. The pings come from 0.4.2 games; the distance is worked out on each server, and players' addresses never leave it.
- **The relay says more:** who sent the packets it dropped, and when a game stops registering with the NAT helper while still signed in.
- **Refused reports leave a trace:** a report the server doesn't take (too many in a day, or files it won't accept) is logged with why and how big it was, and shows as a warning on Sessions. Before, the player was told and nobody else.
- **Finished matches aren't lost** while the coordinator is out of reach, and session ids aren't handed out again after a cleanup.
- **The players list follows sign-ins within seconds**, and one person's accounts on several servers show as one row; maps and game modes show by name.
- **Players online** on the Overview no longer drops to zero between the servers' updates.
- **Alerts:** one when every server goes silent at once (the coordinator can't be reached), and one for a server whose launcher API stops answering.
- **Backups:** with `/etc/5th-echelon/backup.env`, the installer streams each database to Cloudflare R2 (seconds behind, a week of history) and archives to Backblaze B2.
- **Coordinator failover:** each server of a group can keep a standby coordinator (`--standby` in the installer). With the coordinator's name proxied through Cloudflare (one level below your domain, which Cloudflare's free certificate covers), when its machine is down for 3 minutes the next server in line checks the others can't reach it either, restores the coordinator from its live backup and takes over the name; the replaced server stands down when it's back, and moving the coordinator home is one command. Setting it up: [docs/failover.md](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced/blob/main/docs/failover.md).
- **A failed update no longer costs the server its rollback:** the updater keeps the copy of the release before aside until the new one comes back healthy. Before, it replaced that copy first, so after one failed update a rollback could only go back to the release already running.
- **Each server checks its own programs before it starts:** the updater, as root, compares the server's and the coordinator's programs with the release's signed checksums (kept from the install, so it works offline). The result is in `install-server.sh --status`, in a **Program** column on the admin UI's Updates page, and a program that doesn't match the signed release raises an alert. It never stops a server starting. Rerun the installer once to turn it on.
- **Caddy waits longer for uploads:** request bodies may take 3 minutes and be 8 MB (were 30 s and 4 MB), so players' reports from far away get through. Rerun the installer to apply it.
- **The server directory** (`/v1/servers`) says when a release is going out (its stage, when it began, and the canary), for players' launchers; servers answer **name checks** (`NameAvailable`) for the launcher, rate limited like sign-ins.
- SQLite in WAL mode, with writes that wait their turn rather than fail when several arrive at once; Uplay's "actions completed" acknowledged; outdated clients retrying a refused sign-in cost the server less.

Connections hold better, false warnings are gone, and your reports get answers. Players who join a public match through the relay keep their connection when their game changes port, the "Other players can't reach you" warning only shows when it's true, and a report you send is never lost to closing the launcher too soon. The admins can now reply to your reports, and you'll see the reply in the launcher. And a security review of the whole network, attacking a copy of it, closed every way it found to take a server down, lock players out or reach them through the relay; the fixes were then tested in real games across two networks.

## Play

Already on 0.4.2? The launcher updates itself. New here? Download **`launcher.exe`** (Windows) or **`launcher-linux-x86_64`** (Linux, Steam Deck) below, run it, press **Connect**, then **Play**. More in [Play in five minutes](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced#play-in-five-minutes).

## What's new

### Playing
- **"Other players can't reach you" only when it's true** (thanks to Oni's report). Leaving your last party, back to the main menu, closes the game's connection for other players until you go online again, which is normal; the overlay took it for a fault after a minute and warned you. Now the game tells the server it's gone offline, and the warning shows only when you're online and the server really doesn't hear from you.
- **Relayed matches keep their connection when your port changes** (thanks to PlaySkill's report). On some mobile and carrier-grade connections, the game's traffic leaves from a different port than the one it registered with the server, and the relay dropped it until the game happened to register again: about 20 seconds without the match, after a restart. The relay now recognises your traffic from any port of your address by its tag, and sends the match's traffic back the way it came. This works with 0.4.2 games too, once your server is on 0.4.3.
- **Choose the key that opens the overlay** (thanks to Icky's report). On most laptops, and some keyboards, the top row sends brightness and media keys unless you hold **Fn**, so F5 did nothing. **Settings › Game** now has the overlay's key (F1 to F12, Insert, Home, End, Page Up or Down, Pause, Scroll Lock or `), and the game reminds you to hold Fn for a function key.
- **Signing in through a VPN works** (Cloudflare WARP and the like). The game's sign-in was refused when its connections left the VPN from neighbouring addresses; a ticket is now good from the address that asked for it, the ones next to it, and any your launcher or game signed in from in the last 10 minutes.
- **The server's details in the overlay wait for a slow connection** (thanks to PlaySkill's report). On mobile connections they timed out about once in a hundred refreshes.
- **The overlay's key only opens it while the game is in front.** It was read from the whole keyboard, so pressing it in a browser over a windowed game opened the panel and kept the mouse inside the browser's window; switching to another program now closes the panel and frees the mouse.
- **A game refused as outdated stops asking.** It used to sign in again every few seconds for as long as it ran; now it waits until you restart it after the launcher has updated.
- **Signing in holds when many players start at once.** The game's sign-in waited for the same few password checks as new accounts and the launcher's sign-ins, and on a busy server it was refused at once ("internal error") rather than wait its turn; it now has a turn of its own.
- **The relay only carries your game's traffic.** It forwards only between players the server put together: in the same party or match, joining a public match's host, or invited by each other. Nobody else can send anything to your game through it.
- **A private match stays private.** A party follows its host into a private match only if they were in the party before the match was made, so someone who added themselves to the host's party can't follow.

### The launcher
- **Support, in the launcher.** A new **Support** page: write to the community network's admins about anything that goes wrong, with the game's or the launcher's log if you tick them (your PC's name, user folder and internet address hidden first). Their answers show there, a notice says when one came, and the overlay tells you in the game. On the community network's servers.
- **Reports aren't lost when you close the launcher** (thanks to PlaySkill's report). Sending a report goes on after you press **Send**; closing the launcher before it finished dropped it without a word. Now a report being sent is kept until the server has it, and sent the next time you start the launcher; closing while one is sending asks whether to wait.
- **Replies to your reports.** The admins can answer a report, and you'll see their reply under **Settings › Feedback › Your reports**, with a notice when one arrives.
- **The 15 minutes before a problem, from the game's log.** When the server sees a problem in your game (a join that failed, your connection dropping, the game restarting in a match), it can ask for the 15 minutes of the game's log before it, at most once an hour (never the whole log), with your PC's name, user folder and internet address hidden first. It's part of the game's diagnostics: **Settings › Feedback** turns them off, and this with them.
- **A refusal that won't change isn't asked again.** An outdated launcher, or a banned account, is told once and asked again only after an update or when you press **Check again**, not every ten minutes.
- **Reports with logs are smaller:** the lines that only trace the game's calls, most of a long log, are left out, so more of a long session fits.
- **The network's servers only over HTTPS.** If one doesn't answer over HTTPS, the launcher stops and says so, rather than send your password over plain HTTP, even the first time you connect (before, only a server you'd used over HTTPS was held to it).
- **IPv6 addresses are hidden too** in reports and diagnostics, as IPv4 ones were.
- **Your server list stays the community one.** Connecting to a server that names its own server directory no longer switches yours to it without asking; the launcher says so, and you can set it in Settings.
- **Your PC's name is hidden whole** in reports and diagnostics (thanks to Oni's report): a PC named after its owner's account kept part of its name.
- **A new account's name needs a letter or a digit:** names of punctuation only (`..`, `-`) are refused.
- **Failed sign-ins from elsewhere don't lock you out:** a sign-in from where your launcher just signed in, or with your identity, isn't held up by someone else guessing your password.

### The game's diagnostics
- **What the game is doing goes too:** when it joins or leaves a game session, when it saves at a checkpoint or a mission's end, and achievements. A few lines a minute at most, private details hidden as before; **Settings › Feedback** turns it off.

### Running servers
- **Light or dark admin UI:** a button at the foot of the rail switches between the system's choice, light and dark (kept in that browser).
- **The world map's day and night show,** in either theme (night was all but invisible on a PC set to light mode), and a player's city shows the time on its clocks, summer time included, not the sun's time from its longitude.
- **Live:** a new page in the admin UI with every game open now on every server: the map and game mode by name, public or private, the host, everyone in it with their ping and whether the relay carries them, and how long it's been going. Each match has its own page with every player's side of it in one list.
- **Players who joined a public match count as in it.** The live numbers, the online lists and friends' presence left out everyone who joined a public match from a search or an invitation: "1 in a match" while three played. The game itself now sees them too, as it did on the original servers: in the player lists of the sessions it finds, when a friend looks for the match they're in, and when it tells friends which match it's in. And two players who joined the same match may be put in touch directly (NAT traversal), which the server refused before.
- **Sessions says how each stay ended:** left, removed by the host (named), the connection dropped, the game restarted or crashed, timed out, the server restarted, or still in it; who else was there and for how long; the map and game mode by name. A game that restarts in the middle of a match is a problem of its own.
- **Fewer false warnings on Sessions:** no "nobody could reach it" for a game still in the menus, or for the old game's registration after a restart, and a game going offline or quitting says so.
- **Game logs:** players' games send their log when something goes wrong (unless they turned the diagnostics off); it's under **Reports › Game logs**, linked from their Sessions row, and isn't counted as an open report.
- **Replies to reports:** a report has a reply box beside the note; the player reads it in their launcher.
- **Support:** a new admin UI page with each player's conversation, their accounts and the logs they sent; answer there, and mark it waiting or resolved. **Message** on a player's page writes to them first (to ask for a log, say). New messages count on the rail and post to the alert webhook (unless report alerts are off). The community network's coordinator only.
- **The game's uploads are taken.** At the end of a session the game uploads its ShadowNet snapshot (loadouts, owned items, purchases, challenge progress), which servers refused until now. Each player's latest is kept and shown to admins on the player's page; nothing changes for players yet.
- **Hardening** from a review of 0.4.2: unreadable requests from games not signed in, and joins named over and over, can no longer fill Sessions; the Sessions page reads at most 100,000 events at once and says when a range has more; a standby no longer takes over from a coordinator that came back while it restored, and a page in front of the coordinator (not the coordinator's answer) no longer counts as it being down; alert webhooks don't follow redirects and show angle brackets plainly (no Slack mentions or links from a server's text).
- **More hardening,** from a review of the whole code: a game's sign-in ticket stops working when the player's password changes, they're banned or the account is deleted (it worked for up to a day); forged packets for connections that don't exist are dropped before the server decrypts them; passwords no longer appear in debug logs; a member server can't fill the coordinator with snapshots or report logs; a wrong-password lockout no longer blocks an admin's passkey; Caddy's admin API is moved off localhost on servers with their own Caddyfile; and the backup jobs run sandboxed.
- **The Roadmap page's Prompt buttons are gone,** with the prompt panel beside **Suggest**.
- **Run the installer again after updating** so Caddy sends the game's uploads to the content server (`/ugc/`); until then they're refused, as before. It also has systemd restart Caddy however often it stops, and warns about a Caddy older than 2.11.7: one connection could crash Caddy 2.11.6 (update it with `apt upgrade caddy`, or let the installer's own build do it).
- **Quieter logs:** two lines every game start sent as warnings (they aren't), and a call without a valid token from an outdated game, logged as an error every time (857 in two days on eu1), now once an hour per address.
- **A refused sign-in is logged once an hour per player,** not every few seconds, while Sessions still counts them all.
- **Admin setup links work again.** A new admin's one-time link opened the sign-in page instead of setup.

### Security review
An attack test of a whole network (a coordinator and servers, as the installer sets them up, then two real servers on the internet), and the fixes for what it found:
- **The launcher's API and the coordinator** cap connections (in all and per address), streams and calls on a connection, and message sizes, with deadlines for slow requests and idle connections closed. One connection could use up a server's memory before.
- **The game service** limits what a connection that hasn't signed in may do (calls a second, connections per address, how long it lives, what it may hold), so a flood without an account can't take a server's CPU, memory or game sign-ins.
- **Without Caddy, ports 80 and 8000** take 1,024 connections and want a request within 3 seconds, so a few addresses holding idle connections can't take every place.
- **The relay** forwards only between players the server put together (above), and looks new pairs up beside the relay, so a flood of them can't slow known ones. **`[nat] relay_pairs = false`** relays between any registered players, as before ([server settings](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced/blob/main/docs/server-settings.md)).
- **The directory** lists a member only once the server answering at its listed host gives the member's own id (`/api/info`), so a member can't list itself at another server's address; it's checked when the address changes and every 6 hours, and a server that fails hears so in its heartbeat. Only public addresses are asked: `coordinator --check-private-hosts` for a LAN or test network, `--no-host-check` to turn it off. A member's players online is held to believable numbers.
- **Support** messages and reads are signed for the coordinator's own name (`--name`, which the installer passes), so they can't be replayed to another; each player's files are capped (32 MB, and 32 MB a day per address), the oldest resolved files make room past the 1 GB in all, and admins can delete a conversation. The name admins see on support messages, suggestions and leaderboards is the one the player linked by, not what their launcher says.
- **Admin sign-in:** no authenticator or recovery code is taken while an account waits after failed tries, recovery codes have the same few tries, and a new sign-in ends the admin's half-finished ones. The audit log also records removing a server, a new join token, an account locked after failures, and an admin downloading a player's file.
- **Reports and joins:** a player's daily reports are counted as they're queued, so many sent at once can't all pass; reports of failed joins are capped; players' words in alerts can't make links.
- **Backups** copy the coordinator's files through a copy only root can touch, so a link in the coordinator's folder can't carry root's keys to the bucket.
- **Releases:** the server's Docker image is signed with the release (its digest, after checking it was built from the release's tag). `scripts/verify-image.sh v0.4.3` checks it and prints the image to run by digest. The installer no longer suggests `--allow-unsigned` for a release with no signature.
- **Rollouts** pick the canary among equally quiet servers by a hash, so a member can't name itself to be first.

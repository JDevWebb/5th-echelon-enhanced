# 0.5.2 "Clear Line"

**0.5.2 is about getting into matches and keeping them fair.** Joins that kept failing for some players are now caught by the server and sent through the relay, players whose network blocks the game's traffic can play over HTTPS, and the game's fair-play checks now compare each player's match rules and weapons with the host's. The launcher updates by itself.

## Getting into matches

- **A router that says it forwards the game's port, but lets nothing in, no longer leaves you unreachable.** Your game's port forwarding (UPnP) made the server treat you as reachable directly, even when a firewall on your PC or a second router dropped everything: nobody could join you, and you could join nobody. The server now checks from another address that the forwarded port lets something in, and if nothing comes back within seconds, your matches go through the server's relay. The game waits for that check before it takes its address, so other players reach it the right way from the start. It checks again every half hour, and whenever your router's forwarding changes.
- **Joins that keep failing move you to the relay.** If your joins fail with two different hosts within ten minutes while other players got in, your games go through the server for the rest of the session, and the game's overlay says so, with how to choose **Always through the server** for good. A host two players couldn't join, who got in elsewhere, moves the same way. One host's failures alone never move anyone.
- **When the relay can't hear your game at all, its traffic goes through HTTPS by itself.** If the server's relay hears nothing from your game for 20 seconds after it signs in (or stops hearing it for 50), the game sends that traffic through the server over a secure connection instead, and you stay joinable. Seen with two PCs behind one router: once one PC's traffic went out on the game's port, the other PC's port forwarding of the same port got nothing through, whatever port it asked for. While the game is still waiting to learn its own address, that takes a second, not 20, and the game is given the relay's address from the start; before, it could be given the other PC's, and hosts turned that player away.
- **Two PCs behind one router** can both play on the same relay. The second PC's port forwarding collided with the first PC's traffic, and it never got through to the relay; the server now spots that and has it forward another port.
- **Where the game's traffic is blocked** (some mobile data, some networks), **the game goes through HTTPS instead.** The launcher checks at every start: when the server answers over HTTPS but the game's traffic (UDP) gets nowhere, the game sends it through the server over a secure connection, and the launcher says so. It's slower than usual, so it's only for when nothing else works. **Settings › Network › Always go through HTTPS** turns it on for a connection that loses most of the game's traffic without blocking it.
- **The connection test no longer fails "Game service sign-in" for a player who hasn't played today.** The server lets the game sign in only after the game itself checked in, so the test's own sign-in was refused with the right password; it now says the server answers.
- **The connection test** (Settings › Network) also names a VPN adapter that's on and may carry your matches (Radmin VPN, Hamachi, ZeroTier, Tailscale, WireGuard...), another program holding the game's match port, and a Windows Firewall rule keeping the game out (Windows writes one when its "Allow access" prompt is dismissed). **Allow the game** fixes that one: after Windows asks for your permission once, the game is let in and the rules blocking it are turned off.

- **"New match" no longer lands you in the lobby of someone who invited you.** An invitation you hadn't accepted answered your next quick match searches for five minutes, even after you asked for a new match, so you were put in the inviter's lobby all the same. Asking for a new match now drops the invitations waiting for you, and quick match ignores invitations while the ask holds; an invitation you accept from the overlay still works.
- **Fewer joins into a room you weren't joining.** That same leftover invitation sent your quick match into a room long after you'd gone elsewhere, and the join failed, sometimes several times in a row. Invitations waiting for you now go once you join a room, and an invitation whose room can't be joined (it's locked, or the game was joining another) goes after the first try.
- **Play SvM and Play Coop no longer go into a search of their own.** Enter or A pressed while the game started (to skip the intro videos, which these already skip) reached the game's menus the moment it arrived in the lobby, and picked Quick match and then the first mode, Classic; pressed on, it left each match it hosted and searched again. The game now ignores the keyboard, mouse and controller for a moment after it reaches the lobby, and a button still held then until it's let go.
- **Play SvM and Play Coop no longer act on your first key.** After the game reached the lobby, the first key you let go (F5 for the overlay too, or an arrow key) picked whatever was highlighted, so you went from the Spies vs Mercs menu into Quick match, and on into a Classic search. The title's Start, which the quick start presses for you, was left waiting for its key to be let go; the quick start now ends with a real Enter on the lobby it chose, so nothing is left waiting.
- **The overlay's start notice** (who you're signed in as, and F5 for friends and invites) shows when Play SvM or Play Coop reaches the lobby. It waited for your first key, or ended while the game was still getting online.
- **Play SvM and Play Coop no longer leave the title music playing.** It was the soundtrack of the title screen's background video, which a normal start ends on the way to the menus; the quick start went past it, so it looped through the lobby and on. It now stops when the game reaches the lobby.
- **A search that finds nothing because every match is under way says so.** A round can't be joined once it's started, so with everyone in one match mid-round your quick match only ever said it was searching. The overlay now says the match is under way, and that picking it in the Matches tab takes you in when its round ends.
- **Still set up on an old server of the community network** (eu1 above all, whose name stopped answering after the move to one server), the launcher said it couldn't connect. It now moves you to the network's server at start, with your name and identity, as it does for the others.

## Maintenance

- **When something's wrong across the network, the launcher says the admins know.** A notice above Play ("Known issue: we're looking into connection problems", "Fixed: matches connect again", news of a hotfix on its way) reaches every launcher within a minute, and players in a match see it once in the game's overlay. **Close** hides it in the launcher until it's changed.
- **When the servers are closed for maintenance, the launcher says so** ("Closed for maintenance until 9:00 pm", in your time) instead of failing to sign in, and tries again every minute. Support still works. The game's overlay warns you in the last ten minutes that you'll be signed out when it starts.

## Fair play

All report only, as before: people decide, nothing bans anyone on its own.
- **The match's rules and weapons are compared between the games in a match.** Every player's game reads the match's rules (time limit, rounds, lives, hack points and timings, respawn times, and the detection and radar ranges) and the stats of every weapon in it (clips, damage boost and range, falloff, rate of fire, reloads, bullet speed). They should be the same in every game; one that differs from the host's was edited in that game, and the admins see which.
- **A Spies vs Mercs match's rules are compared with what the game's own menus offer.** A time limit (3 to 20 minutes), score limit (1 to 3 hacks) or hack time (its four speeds) no menu offers can only come from an edited game: the admins see which, naming the host. Every value the menus offer stays the host's choice.
- **The game checks your own character for cheat switches** while you play a match: god mode, invincibility, invisibility, ghost mode, blocked damage and unlimited inventory. No normal match turns any of them on; if one is on, the admins see which.
- **Reports of changes to the game's code are clearer for the admins.** Each carries a short fingerprint of the whole set of changes, so players running the same tool show the same one, and a change that jumps into memory no program owns (how widescreen fixes, trainers and Cheat Engine scripts patch the game as it runs) is named as such. The add-on's own hooks were sometimes reported as someone else's change; they aren't any more.
- **The add-on's checks report while you play a match**, and the admins see a game whose checks stopped in one, or whose add-on was changed while it ran.

## And

- **A crash while the game loads part of a map in the background** (seen on Silo, an hour or more into a session, in both DirectX 9 and 11): when a part couldn't be opened, the game used it anyway and crashed. It now tries again on the next frames, which gets past a part that couldn't be opened for a moment; a part still missing after ten seconds goes on as before, and the log names it so the cause can be found.
- **The launcher can start with Windows** (Settings › Display › Starting), so friends and invitations reach you: **minimised**, or **in the tray**, with no window until you click its icon (its menu opens the launcher or quits it; opening the launcher again from its shortcut shows that one). Off unless you turn it on.
- **Long answers from the server** (over 2,000 bytes) went out with their parts numbered backwards, which a game drops; they're now numbered as the game expects. The server's log shows which calls send them.

- **The old launcher's code is gone.** The egui launcher, kept since 0.5.0 as a fallback nobody was given, is removed with its build; the launcher is the one you have.

### Running servers
- **Maintenance windows can close a server (or the whole network) to everyone but testers.** Updates › Maintenance › Who can play: **Only testers** (ticked by default for new windows; windows booked before stay notices only). Sign-ins and the API are refused to everyone else while it's under way, and the players on are signed out as it starts. Make a tester on a player's page (Moderation › During maintenance). The server keeps the windows in `maintenance.json`, so the gate holds through a restart while the coordinator is down.
- **Reboots for the system's updates happen only when nobody is on the server**: in its quiet hours (an admin's, set on the server's page, or learned from four weeks of play), or in a booked window. The server drains for 20 seconds, checks again, and has its updater reboot it. A reboot pending over three days is flagged on the server's page, with **Reboot when empty, any hour**.
- **System updates no longer restart the server mid-match.** On Ubuntu, a library update had needrestart restart the server and coordinator at once, dropping every game on them (de1, 14 Nov). The installer now keeps needrestart off the 5th Echelon services: they take the new libraries at their next update or reboot, which the server times.
- **The tunnel** for players whose UDP is blocked: Caddy proxies `/tunnel` on 443 to the server (127.0.0.1:21130), and `/api/info` says `"tunnel": true`.
- **Notices to every player** (Updates › Notices to every player): post a one-line notice (a known problem, news, or a fix), edit or end it; it can end by itself after a few hours or days. Three live at most; every launcher shows them above Play within a minute, and each server passes them to its games' overlay (`/api/info`, `notices`) to show once. Each post, edit and end is in the audit log.
- **The admin UI:** a player's **Fair play** tab lists what their game's add-on reported (game code, clock speed, rules, weapons, heartbeat), repeats folded; **Players** finds a player by the name the network knows them by too; Sessions says why each player is relayed (asked, no forwarded port, router behind another, forwarded port unreachable, joins kept failing) and when a player plays through the tunnel.
- **The relays** check a forwarded port from another member of the network, through the signed link (one check a second to an address at most).

## Updating to 0.5.2

**Players:** the launcher updates by itself.

**Servers:** the rollout installs 0.5.2's programs as usual. **Then run `install-server.sh` once on each machine** (as for any change of settings): it adds the tunnel's route to Caddy and the server's setting for it, and hands reboots to the server (it turns off unattended upgrades' own reboots, and removes the weekly reboot timer some machines got on 13 November). Until then those two stay as they were, and everything else works.

As with every release, a server on 0.5.2 takes 0.5.2 games and launchers only (`[clients] minimum_version` defaults to the server's own release).

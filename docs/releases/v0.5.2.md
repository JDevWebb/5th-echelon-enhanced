# 0.5.2 "Clear Line"

**0.5.2 is about getting into matches and keeping them fair.** Joins that kept failing for some players are now caught by the server and sent through the relay, players whose network blocks the game's traffic can play over HTTPS, and the game's fair-play checks now compare each player's match rules and weapons with the host's. The launcher updates by itself.

## Getting into matches

- **A router that says it forwards the game's port, but lets nothing in, no longer leaves you unreachable.** Your game's port forwarding (UPnP) made the server treat you as reachable directly, even when a firewall on your PC or a second router dropped everything: nobody could join you, and you could join nobody. The server now checks from another address that the forwarded port lets something in, and if nothing comes back within seconds, your matches go through the server's relay. It checks again every half hour, and whenever your router's forwarding changes.
- **Joins that keep failing move you to the relay.** If your joins fail with two different hosts within ten minutes while other players got in, your games go through the server for the rest of the session, and the game's overlay says so, with how to choose **Always through the server** for good. A host two players couldn't join, who got in elsewhere, moves the same way. One host's failures alone never move anyone.
- **Two PCs behind one router** can both play on the same relay. The second PC's port forwarding collided with the first PC's traffic, and it never got through to the relay; the server now spots that and has it forward another port.
- **Where the game's traffic is blocked** (some mobile data, some networks), **the game goes through HTTPS instead.** The launcher checks at every start: when the server answers over HTTPS but the game's traffic (UDP) gets nowhere, the game sends it through the server over a secure connection, and the launcher says so. It's slower than usual, so it's only for when nothing else works. **Settings › Network › Always go through HTTPS** turns it on for a connection that loses most of the game's traffic without blocking it.
- **The connection test** (Settings › Network) also names a VPN adapter that's on and may carry your matches (Radmin VPN, Hamachi, ZeroTier, Tailscale, WireGuard...), and another program holding the game's match port.

- **Still set up on an old server of the community network** (eu1 above all, whose name stopped answering after the move to one server), the launcher said it couldn't connect. It now moves you to the network's server at start, with your name and identity, as it does for the others.

## Maintenance

- **When something's wrong across the network, the launcher says the admins know.** A notice above Play ("Known issue: we're looking into connection problems", "Fixed: matches connect again", news of a hotfix on its way) reaches every launcher within a minute, and players in a match see it once in the game's overlay. **Close** hides it in the launcher until it's changed.
- **When the servers are closed for maintenance, the launcher says so** ("Closed for maintenance until 9:00 pm", in your time) instead of failing to sign in, and tries again every minute. Support still works. The game's overlay warns you in the last ten minutes that you'll be signed out when it starts.

## Fair play

All report only, as before: people decide, nothing bans anyone on its own.
- **The match's rules and weapons are compared between the games in a match.** Every player's game reads the match's rules (time limit, rounds, lives, hack points and timings, respawn times, and the detection and radar ranges) and the stats of every weapon in it (clips, damage boost and range, falloff, rate of fire, reloads, bullet speed). They should be the same in every game; one that differs from the host's was edited in that game, and the admins see which.
- **The game checks your own character for cheat switches** while you play a match: god mode, invincibility, invisibility, ghost mode, blocked damage and unlimited inventory. No normal match turns any of them on; if one is on, the admins see which.
- **The add-on's checks report while you play a match**, and the admins see a game whose checks stopped in one, or whose add-on was changed while it ran.

## And

- **The launcher can start with Windows** (Settings › Display › Starting), minimised, so friends and invitations reach you. Off unless you turn it on.
- **Long answers from the server** (over 2,000 bytes) went out with their parts numbered backwards, which a game drops; they're now numbered as the game expects. The server's log shows which calls send them.

### Running servers
- **Maintenance windows can close a server (or the whole network) to everyone but testers.** Updates › Maintenance › Who can play: **Only testers** (ticked by default for new windows; windows booked before stay notices only). Sign-ins and the API are refused to everyone else while it's under way, and the players on are signed out as it starts. Make a tester on a player's page (Moderation › During maintenance). The server keeps the windows in `maintenance.json`, so the gate holds through a restart while the coordinator is down.
- **Reboots for the system's updates happen only when nobody is on the server**: in its quiet hours (an admin's, set on the server's page, or learned from four weeks of play), or in a booked window. The server drains for 20 seconds, checks again, and has its updater reboot it. A reboot pending over three days is flagged on the server's page, with **Reboot when empty, any hour**.
- **The tunnel** for players whose UDP is blocked: Caddy proxies `/tunnel` on 443 to the server (127.0.0.1:21130), and `/api/info` says `"tunnel": true`.
- **Notices to every player** (Updates › Notices to every player): post a one-line notice (a known problem, news, or a fix), edit or end it; it can end by itself after a few hours or days. Three live at most; every launcher shows them above Play within a minute, and each server passes them to its games' overlay (`/api/info`, `notices`) to show once. Each post, edit and end is in the audit log.
- **The admin UI:** a player's **Fair play** tab lists what their game's add-on reported (game code, clock speed, rules, weapons, heartbeat), repeats folded; **Players** finds a player by the name the network knows them by too; Sessions says why each player is relayed (asked, no forwarded port, router behind another, forwarded port unreachable, joins kept failing) and when a player plays through the tunnel.
- **The relays** check a forwarded port from another member of the network, through the signed link (one check a second to an address at most).

## Updating to 0.5.2

**Players:** the launcher updates by itself.

**Servers:** the rollout installs 0.5.2's programs as usual. **Then run `install-server.sh` once on each machine** (as for any change of settings): it adds the tunnel's route to Caddy and the server's setting for it, and hands reboots to the server (it turns off unattended upgrades' own reboots, and removes the weekly reboot timer some machines got on 13 November). Until then those two stay as they were, and everything else works.

As with every release, a server on 0.5.2 takes 0.5.2 games and launchers only (`[clients] minimum_version` defaults to the server's own release).

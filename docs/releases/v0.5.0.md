# 0.5.0 "Rules of Engagement"

Every op has its rules, and until now nobody on our servers was checking them. This one gives the community network a referee who watches from every seat: bans that hold for the PC, checks that report a sped-up clock or changed code, other players' games noticing someone moving faster than the game allows, and match recordings the admins can replay when you report a player. Around it: a redrawn launcher, lobbies that carry on when their host leaves, choosing your own match, and one matchmaking server for the whole network.

Also on this op:
- **A new launcher**, redrawn in the Field ops look, with a Play button that says whether you're ready and why not.
- **The lobby carries on when its host leaves**, and a party follows its leader into a friend's private match.
- **Matches**, a tab of its own: every public match on your server in full (who's in it, its host, parties and friends, the sides once a round runs, its state and settings, roughly how far it is), to join one, watch one for a seat, or start a new one.
- **A Friends page** for friends, requests and blocked players.
- **Report a player** from the overlay, with the match attached.
- **Rubberbanding, hits not landing and joins timing out** in long sessions: a bug in 0.4.6's crash fix, found and fixed.
- **Four crashes** in the game's own code patched, one of them on the way back to the lobby.
- **The game starts without Ubisoft Connect** on Windows too, as it always has on Linux.

## Play

Already on 0.4.6 or 0.4.7? The launcher updates itself. New here? Download **`launcher.exe`** (Windows) or **`launcher-linux-x86_64`** (Linux, Steam Deck) below, run it, press **Connect**, then **Play**. More in [Play in five minutes](https://github.com/JDevWebb/5th-echelon-enhanced#play-in-five-minutes).

On a Steam Deck in Game Mode the launcher opens full screen and works with the controller.

Start the game from the launcher (or through Steam with the launcher's **Start the game through Steam**): the launcher is what updates the game's add-on. Servers on 0.5.0 take games and launchers on 0.5.0 only (unless their admins set otherwise), and an outdated game now says how to update.

## What's new

### The launcher
- **Redrawn**, in the Field ops look: the game's art on the Play screen, with your server, friends and news beside it; every screen easier to read, and to use with a keyboard and a screen reader. Every screen is in one side menu (Play, Matches, Friends, Servers, Chat, Intel, then Support and Settings, with GitHub and Discord), the same on Windows, Linux and the Steam Deck: one launcher, one file, nothing else to install. In a **dark or a light look** (Settings › Display, or the button in the title bar; *As the system* follows Windows' or the desktop's), with 5th Echelon Enhanced's own mark. Built anew in [Slint](https://slint.dev): one native window, quick to start, full screen in the Deck's Game Mode.
- **The overlay matches:** the panel in the game (F5) takes the launcher's look, dark or light, with the same type and the same words: its tabs have the launcher's icons, and each tab is laid out as the launcher's pages are, in cards. Its first toast (how to open it) now waits until you're past the game's intro videos, where it used to show and go unseen.
- **The window fits your screen:** on a laptop or a 720p screen it opens no taller than the screen leaves room for, centred.
- **The Play button says where you stand:** PLAY and "Ready to play"; amber with the warnings; red when something needs fixing, the button then being the fix ("Set up a save", "Fix 2 things"); "Server updating" with when, during a release. The words say it as well as the colour.
- **Friends** (the side menu): your friends, online first and what they're doing, with Join or Invite; requests to answer and those you sent; finding players; and the players you blocked, with Unblock. Remove and Block ask first and say what they do. A friend playing on another server has **Switch server**, which sets you up there with your identity and your friends.
- **Play waits until the game is ready:** greyed out with a spinner while the launcher checks (**Checking…**) and, the first time, while it notes the game's files for your sign-in (**Checking game files…**, a few seconds; after that only a file that changed is read again).
- **Intel:** the server's news and the roadmap, on one screen.
- **Without Ubisoft Connect, by default** (Settings › Game): the game starts without opening Ubisoft Connect on Windows, as it already did on Linux and the Steam Deck; saves and achievements work as before. Ubisoft's own overlay is what you lose: turn **Start without Ubisoft Connect** off to have it back.
- **Which DirectX it ran with:** Settings › Game shows the DirectX the game last ran with under Renderer, and says why when it isn't the one you chose (Steam, a shortcut or Ubisoft Connect started it).
- **Sign-ins are tried again only when it can help:** a server out of reach after a growing wait (5 s up to 5 minutes); wrong details three times, then not for 30 minutes; an outdated launcher not until it's updated.

### Matches
- **The lobby carries on when its host leaves** (thanks to mpw, who wrote it, #8). When the host of a public match's lobby leaves, or drops from a running public match, one of the other players takes the lobby over and everyone stays together, instead of each landing back in their own lobby. Private matches end as before.
- **A party follows its leader into a friend's private match:** a leader who accepts an invitation keeps the party, and the members who are the host's friends come along; the others still need the host's own invitation. After the match the party comes back together.
- Host migration is **part of every game**, on DirectX 9 and 11: there's nothing to turn on or off. A party whose leader leaves carries on under the member who takes over, and invitations to a lobby that moved go to its new one; the server does that for everyone.
- **Matches** (the side menu, or **Choose a match…** beside Play): every public match on your server, updated live, with its mode and map, its host, players out of seats, what it's doing (*In lobby · waiting for 3 more*, *Round 2 under way · 6 min*, *Between rounds*) and your ping to it, over the map's own picture from your copy of the game. The header counts everyone on the server. Pick one for who's in it (the host, who came as a party, every player's ping; spies and mercs once a round runs, from the host's game), the match settings, and **Join this match**; one under way offers **Join when this round ends**, and a full one **Join when a seat opens**: a place in the match's queue (your place shows; at most 8 wait), and when the match can take players the first in line go in, before anyone who just presses Join. Filters for the mode, matches under way and full ones, friends first, and sorted by ping, players or age. A match with someone you blocked isn't listed, nor one with someone who blocked you; private matches are only counted. **New match** starts one of your own whatever is open (searches skip the open matches until you host). The overlay's Match tab still lists the open matches in the game. On a server before 0.5.0 the tab shows the open matches without names.
- **The launcher keeps room for the full layout:** its window is never smaller than the full view at your size (Settings › Display); on a screen too small for that, the size is lowered to fit, instead of squeezing the pages.
- **Quick match leaves out matches with someone you've blocked** in them, without a word to anyone.
- **Searches skip matches whose round has started:** the host's game now tells the server when its round or mission is under way, so searches, Join and a friend's Join leave it out until it's over. And a search turned away by a match under way no longer gets offered the same one again.

### Fair play (community network)
Everything new is **report only**: the network's admins see it and judge it, and nothing bans anyone on its own.
- **Report a player:** the overlay's Match tab lists who's in your match, and who was in your matches of the last 30 minutes, each with **Report**: why (cheating, griefing, their name, something else) and what happened. It reaches the admins with your session's log and the match. One report a player every 6 hours.
- **Bans hold across the network and for the PC.** A ban reaches every account of the player's identity on every server. The game and the launcher send hashes of the PC's hardware ids at sign-in (never the ids themselves); a new account on a banned player's PC is banned as long as that ban runs. Ids that many players share (a virtual machine's, a manufacturer's placeholders, Wine's) are never matched, and a Steam Deck is told apart by its own ids, not Wine's. Kept 180 days after last seen, or while a ban runs, and deleted with the account.
- **The game's clocks:** the add-on compares the clocks a speed hack changes with the time Windows keeps, and reports a game running fast.
- **The game's code:** two minutes in, and every 30 s after, the add-on compares the game's code with its executable, and reports a change that isn't 5th Echelon's, Fusion Fix's or another known mod's.
- **The game's files:** when the game signs in, the add-on sends one-way hashes of the game's own files that change play (both executables, the game's DLLs, Ubisoft's DLL, the script and data packages; a few hundred MB, hashed once and remembered: the first time, Play waits a few seconds on **Checking game files**) and the names of other such files next to them (a loose `.ini` override, a mod's DLL). The **Sessions** page shows a player whose copy of a file few others share (an edited executable or package, a crack's DLL), once enough players said that file. Nothing outside the game's folder is looked at.
- **Witness reports:** your game watches how fast the other players' characters move and tells your server of any faster than the game allows. One player's word is nothing: the admins see it only once two players on different PCs and networks saw it in two matches. On the community network it is part of playing and can't be turned off.
- **Match recordings:** your game records each match (where every character was, faced and shot, deaths, kills and the round) in the game folder's `replays`, the newest 20 kept, about 1-2 MB a match. Each one of a minute or more also goes to your server, which keeps it a week, for the admins to watch when a match is reported. On the community network recordings are part of playing and can't be turned off; on a server of your own, `ReplayRecording = false` and `ReplayUpload = false` in `uplay.toml` turn them off or keep them on your PC. Beside each one the game keeps the match's network traffic (on your PC only, the newest 10, research towards replaying whole matches in the game itself); `NetCapture = false` turns that off. On DirectX 9 and 11.
- **On the server:** stats far from the network's normal (a gain no one makes in that time, a record far past everyone's), a host changing the round's settings while it runs (a respawn time of 500 s, the 2013 trick), a host who keeps ending matches with players in them within two minutes of the round starting, and a relayed player's sending going silent then bursting (a lag switch) are listed for the admins. None of it changes anything for players.

### Linux
- **Proton prefixes made outside Steam** (`umu-run`, or Lutris and Heroic with a Proton runner) now start with `umu-run`, in the Proton that made them, instead of the system's Wine, which could hang the game. Without umu-launcher installed the launcher says so, and the checklist suggests installing it. Thanks to mpw (#10).
- **A game folder outside its Wine prefix** (plain Wine, the prefix in `WINEPREFIX` or `~/.wine`): the launcher now finds the save where Wine keeps it, instead of saying "No save game yet" and blocking Play.
- **Brighten the picture works on Linux:** the game's own brightness does nothing under Proton, so on Linux the launcher brightens the picture with vkBasalt (mid-tones lighter, black and white kept; Home turns it off and on in the game). It needs vkBasalt's 32-bit layer (`lib32-vkbasalt` on Arch). When Steam starts the game, the launcher says what to put in its Launch Options. Thanks to mpw (#11).

## What's fixed

### Crashes
The add-on patches each of these in memory as the game starts (the game's files are never changed), on DirectX 9 and 11, and leaves a game build it doesn't recognise alone. `DisableCrashFixes = true` in `uplay.toml` turns them all off, should one ever be the problem.
- **Destroying tagging devices** (thanks to Ronis). When a spy destroyed mercs' tagging devices with EMP or the overcharge suit, the game could go on using part of a device that was already gone, and crash; when it was the host's game, the match ended for everyone. The game now leaves out what's gone.
- **Back to the lobby after a match** (thanks to mpw, who found it and wrote the fix, #12; and to Gex and Sokolev). The game could crash on the way back, most often after Play SvM. It reads its packages in an order it recorded when it last loaded them, and skipping the Paladin hub meant the lobby's was never recorded. A read the recorded order can't place now comes straight from the package files. `DisableUmdReadFix = true` turns only this one off.
- **Co-op, a minute or two after a mission's end** (thanks to Glacio1 and AngledCircles). The enemies check whether each player is in one of their zones, and read the player's character without checking it's there; while a player had none for a moment, the game crashed. A player without a character is now simply not in the zone.
- **A player's navigation disrupted while they hold no item** (a jammer, an EMP; thanks to Ghost_Leader): it read something the game had already cleared; it's checked first now.
- **Crash details in the game's log:** after the line a crash already wrote, the registers, the code bytes where it happened and what each register points to, its callers by the saved frame pointers, and what the game was doing (in a match, the mode and map, how long since its last save); a line when nothing handled it and the game is closing on it; freezes say their callers too. The overlay says when it draws its first frame, and warns when it hasn't drawn one in a minute with the game in front (its key then does nothing: another overlay drawing first, usually), from two players' "F5 does nothing" reports.
- **A game that closed at start (0xC0000142)** (thanks to EitherBat2025) when Ubisoft's `uplay_r1_loader.orig.dll` had version information Windows couldn't read: the add-on panicked; now it notes it and goes on.

### Playing
- **Rubberbanding, hits not landing, and joins timing out in long sessions (0.4.6 and 0.4.7)** (thanks to Tea_bag, Cloud, Ghost_Leader, curryman and omar). 0.4.6's fix for a crash in the game's network code checks, before a sent packet's news (arrived, or lost) is passed on, that whatever waits on it is still alive, and drops what isn't. It took everything above 2 GB of the game's memory for dead, but the game uses up to 4 GB, and in a long session most of what waits on packets is up there. So the game never heard its packets had arrived: movement and hits went late or not at all, the other players saw you jump back, and hosts' games waited on joins that had come through. It also logged every drop (one game wrote 1.94 million lines, a 500 MB log), which made the game stutter by itself. Now only what really isn't the game's is dropped, and the log says so 16 times, then less and less often. Players on 0.4.6 or 0.4.7 seeing this can set `DisableCrashFixes = true` in the game folder's `uplay.toml` until they update, which also turns off that release's other crash fixes.
- **A join that can't work now says why** (thanks to Sem). When your game can't sign up with the server's NAT helper (a VPN or a firewall usually stops it), players who play through the server's relay can't reach you, nor you them. The game now tells you so once, and a join that fails for that reason says it, instead of "try again".
- **Lobby chat after joining an open match** (thanks to Gex). A player who joined someone's match from a search (rather than by invitation) was still in their own party too, made after the match, and their Lobby room was that party: nobody else read what they wrote, and they saw nothing from the match. Lobby is now always the match you're in, and your party only outside one.
- **A game started again is let in straight away** (thanks to omar and Agent1), instead of waiting up to a minute ("its signatures are all in use") for its old connection to the server to time out.
- **Two PCs behind one router, on the same server:** the game always reached the servers from port 3074, and some routers drop the second PC's connection when another already holds that port to the same server ("Splinter Cell service is unavailable" after signing in). The game now uses a port the system picks; `FixedServerPort = true` under `[Networking]` in `uplay.toml` keeps 3074 (a router forwarded to it by hand).
- **Esc after stopping the launcher's navigation:** Esc that stopped the game finding its way to your match was kept from the game for the rest of the session, so the game's menu opened only some of the time. Only that one press is kept back now.
- **"Not connected" in the overlay after a server restart** stayed up until the next invitation, though everything worked again: it goes as soon as the server answers.
- **An outdated game says how to update:** started from Steam or Ubisoft Connect it keeps its old add-on, so press Play in the launcher once. When your launcher is up to date and running, the message says exactly that, with **Start the game through Steam** for those who start from Steam.

## Running servers
- **Global matchmaking** (the community network moves to it in this release's update window):
  - A server can be a **NAT helper and relay alone** (`[role] kind = "relay"`, the installer's `--role relay`), near a region's players: no game services, listed as a relay, never a place to play.
  - The network's **matchmaking server** (`[matchmaking] network_relays`, the installer's `--matchmaking`, named to the coordinator with `--matchmaking-server`) offers the network's relays; the launcher probes each and has the game use the nearest.
  - The matchmaking server signs a **relay pass** at sign-in that relays elsewhere take (`[role] ticket_key`, the installer's `--ticket-key`). Passes and NAT tickets are bound to the game's own session key, so one copied off the wire is no use.
  - Relays **forward to each other** over signed links the coordinator sets up, only between players the matchmaking server put together, and send their players' session events to it.
  - **The whole network moves at once** ([cutover.md](../cutover.md)): `dedicated_server --merge-accounts` merges a server's player accounts into the matchmaking server's by identity (friends, blocks, bans, uploads and stats with them), `coordinator copy-links FROM TO` then gives the moved players their links on the matchmaking server (without them their friends don't load there), and a server that became a relay says where its players moved (`moved_to`), so launchers move their players by themselves, only to a server of the same network.
  - **The matchmaking server has a standby** on each relay (`--mm-standby`, `--mm-record`): its name DNS only, a takeover restores its database, key files and settings from its backup (backup.sh now keeps a game server's) and runs it in the relay's place as the same server; standing down puts the relay back ([failover.md](../failover.md)).
  - **Rollouts know roles:** a relay is never the canary, updates only when it carries nobody, and the matchmaking server goes last.
- **Far rooms in quick match** (`[matchmaking] far_ms`, off by default): results nearest first, and hosts farther than `far_ms` left out while a nearer one matches (`far_strict`: always). How far is measured where it can be: each game signs in with its launcher's round trip to every relay, and two players are as far apart as the least sum over a relay both reached; otherwise from where they are. The matchmaking server tells relays which players it put together as it does (a search's first hosts, a probe, a join), so the game's quick pings across relays aren't held up asking.
- **Choosing a match** (`choose-match` in `/api/info`): `NewMatch` and `JoinRoom` in the player API; `/api/playing`'s matches carry their room and whether they're under way.
- **Fair play in the admin UI:** a ban is network-wide by identity and holds for the PC (the player page says what a PC shares, never the ids); the **Leaderboards** tab lists flagged gains and records, with **Remove stats**, each named in the game's own words (*Kills · SvM Classic · Spies vs Mercs, per game mode*), and its mode and mission lists name every mode, mission and ladder; the **Sessions** page marks round-settings changes during a round, lag-switch rhythms, clock and code reports, witness reports once two independent witnesses agree, signs of a banned player back under a new name (flags only), and what each search left out and why.
- **Players' reports** arrive with the match and the server's log of the reporter's session (Reports), and as `player_report` session events.
- **Replays:**
  - **Match recordings** from players' games are kept in the server's `data/recordings` for a week, at most 3 GB (the oldest go first), one a minute and 40 a day a player, each at most 7.5 MB. The coordinator gets one only when an admin opens it from the Sessions page (**Open replay**), keeps it 14 days (at most 4 GB) in its data folder's `recordings`, never in its database.
  - **Replays in 3D** (Sessions › a match recording › Open, then **3D**): the match among the map's real walls, floors and props, each player as a figure in their team's colour with where they faced and shot, cut away above a height to see inside, and a camera to fly or follow a player. The map comes from your own copy of the game, never from the network: run **`map-geometry.exe "<game folder>" <out folder>`** (it's with the release) once, which writes a `.femg` file for each Spies vs Mercs map (about 3-4 MB, a second each), and load the map's file on the Replay page; the browser keeps it. Keep those files to yourself: they're made from the game.
  - **Whole-match replays** (a match on the Sessions page › **Replay the whole match**): every player's recording of the match played together, each player as their own game recorded them, the others filled in from whoever saw them best; the table says, for each player, how far the others' games saw them from their own recording (a doctored recording, or rubberbanding, shows as metres).
- **Federation:** a server moved to another name of the same coordinator carries on there with its secret, without the join token (in 0.4.6, a moved server stayed unjoined until given it).
- **Chat:** a player holds at most 3 waits for messages at once; after a coordinator failover, servers pick chat up where it was; a player's Lobby is the one they're in when messages arrive.
- **Crashes, from every player** (a new admin page, **Crashes**): games now send the previous run's crashes and freezes when they next start (with the diagnostics agreement, redacted on the PC), so a crash that closed the game is seen, and players who never send a report count. They're grouped by where in the game's code they happened, with the players hit, how often each ended the game, the releases, and the latest one's details: what the game was doing (match, mode, map, time since its last save), the registers and the code there (and what each register points to), and its callers. A group can be marked fixed in a release (and shows when it comes back), known or watching; a new one that hits three players in a day is an alert, as is a fixed one back. The page also shows how often each of the add-on's crash fixes kept a game going, and the builds where one didn't go in. New `Misc.Crash` and `Misc.CrashFixes` calls; `crash` and `crash_fixes` session events.
- **The Sessions page** loads in about a second again: working out each player's timeline copied everything they had so far for every event, minutes of work for a week with a player of tens of thousands of events (366 s of a week's page, now 65 ms), and it was done on the coordinator's request threads, holding servers' calls up meanwhile (now off them).
- **DirectX 9 or 11:** the game says which it runs on when it signs in, and the Sessions page shows the share of players on each (**On DirectX 11**), counting each player once. Games before 0.5.0 don't say.
- **A coordinator behind Cloudflare** (`--coordinator-cert`) now refuses connections that don't come through Cloudflare, as its site always said: the refusal sat beside the site's routes, and Caddy runs those first, so it never ran. Client addresses were never affected (Cloudflare's header was only ever taken from Cloudflare). The ops test now checks the site the installer writes, in Caddy.
- **The installer** warns when a drop-in in `5th-echelon-coordinator.service.d` sets `ExecStart=`, which would override the unit it writes.

## Updating to 0.5.0

**Servers of your own:** update as usual (the rollout, in an update window, or the installer by hand). The new options are all optional: a server that sets none of them runs as before, with its own NAT helper and relay. Once a server runs 0.5.0 it takes 0.5.0 games and launchers only, as `[clients] minimum_version` defaults to the server's own release; launchers update themselves.

**The community network** moves to one matchmaking server in this release's update window, every region at once: everyone signs in to the same server (in Beauharnois, Canada), so every search finds players from every region, and the servers in Europe and Oceania become relays, which the game picks the nearest of by itself. Your account, name, friends, blocks and stats move with you, and the launcher moves you over by itself the first time it starts after the move.

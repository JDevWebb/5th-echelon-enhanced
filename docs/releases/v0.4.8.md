# 0.4.8 (draft: notes are collected here as the release is built; the opening, thanks and Play section come at release)

## What's new

### Fair play (research)
- **Witness reports**: your game watches how fast the other players' characters move and tells your server of any faster than the game allows. One player's word is nothing: the admins see it only once two players on different PCs and networks saw it in two matches. On for everyone; `WitnessReports = false` in `uplay.toml` turns it off.
- **Match recordings**: your game records each match (where every character was, faced and shot, deaths, kills and the round) to the game folder's `replays`, the newest 20 kept, about 1-2 MB a match. Each one of a minute or more also goes to your server, which keeps it a week; the network's admins can watch it top down (where everyone was, faced and shot, and who killed whom) to see what happened in a match someone reported. `ReplayRecording = false` in `uplay.toml` turns recording off, `ReplayUpload = false` keeps recordings on your PC. Beside each one the game keeps the match's network traffic too (on your PC only, the newest 10, research towards replaying whole matches in the game itself); `NetCapture = false` turns that off. DirectX 11 only.

### Running servers
- **Replays in 3D** (Sessions › a match recording › Open, then **3D**): the match among the map's real walls, floors and props, each player as a figure in their team's colour with where they faced and shot, cut away above a height to see inside, and a camera to fly or follow a player. The map comes from your own copy of the game, never from the network: run **`map-geometry.exe "<game folder>" <out folder>`** (it's with the release) once, which writes a `.femg` file for each Spies vs Mercs map (about 3-4 MB, a second each), and load the map's file on the Replay page; the browser keeps it. Keep those files to yourself: they're made from the game.
- **Whole-match replays** (a match on the Sessions page › **Replay the whole match**): every player's recording of the match played together, each player as their own game recorded them, the others filled in from whoever saw them best; the table says, for each player, how far the others' games saw them from their own recording (a doctored recording, or rubberbanding, shows as metres).
- **Match recordings** from players' games are kept in the server's `data/recordings` for a week, at most 3 GB (the oldest go first), one a minute and 40 a day a player, each at most 7.5 MB. The coordinator gets one only when an admin opens it from the Sessions page (Open replay), keeps it 14 days (at most 4 GB) in its data folder's `recordings`, never in its database.

## What's fixed

- **Rubberbanding, hits not landing, and joins timing out in long sessions (0.4.6 and 0.4.7).** 0.4.6's fix for a crash in the game's network code checks, before a sent packet's news (arrived, or lost) is passed on, that whatever waits on it is still alive, and drops what isn't. It took everything above 2 GB of the game's memory for dead, but the game uses up to 4 GB, and in a long session most of what waits on packets is up there. So the game never heard its packets had arrived: movement and hits went late or not at all, the other players saw you jump back, and hosts' games waited on joins that had come through. It also logged every drop (one game wrote 1.94 million lines, a 500 MB log), which made the game stutter by itself. Now only what really isn't the game's is dropped, and the log says so 16 times, then less and less often. Players on 0.4.6 or 0.4.7 seeing this can set `DisableCrashFixes = true` in the game folder's `uplay.toml` until they update, which also turns off that release's other crash fixes.
- **Lobby chat after joining an open match.** A player who joined someone's match from a search (rather than by invitation) was still in their own party too, made after the match, and their Lobby room was that party: nobody else read what they wrote, and they saw nothing from the match. Lobby is now always the match you're in, and your party only outside one.
- **A game that closed at start (0xC0000142)** when Ubisoft's `uplay_r1_loader.orig.dll` had version information Windows couldn't read: the add-on panicked; now it notes it and goes on.
